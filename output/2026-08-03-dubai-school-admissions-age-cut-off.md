## New Placement Guidelines for Dubai Schools

The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) has issued new placement guidelines that will govern school admissions in [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai) starting from the 2026-27 academic year. These guidelines will determine how children are placed in private [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) based on their age, previous school records, and individual learning needs, impacting families planning to enrol their children in Dubai schools.

### Understanding the New Age Cut-Off Rules

The new rules specify that age will be the primary factor for placing children in Pre-KG through KG2 (FS1 to Year 1). For students entering Grade 1 (Year 2) and above, the transfer certificate from their previous school will be the main reference for placement. This change aims to standardise the admission process and ensure that children are placed in grades that are appropriate for their development.

Children entering schools that follow a September academic year must reach the required age by December 31, whereas those in schools commencing in April must meet the age criteria by March 31. For the 2026-27 academic year, these rules will only apply to new registrations within the KHDA system, while children already enrolled or transferring between schools will not be affected.

### Flexibility for New Registrations

A one-time arrangement will allow parents of children born between September 1 and December 31, 2022, to choose between FS1 or FS2 based on their child's readiness. This flexibility is crucial for families whose children are not currently enrolled in any educational institution.

The KHDA guidelines also note that some flexibility may be available for British curriculum schools for FS1 admissions, provided that the child's readiness is formally assessed and documented. However, this flexibility does not extend to other year groups.

### Support for International Students

For families relocating to Dubai, the new guidelines clarify how schools should accommodate children coming from countries where formal education starts later than in the UAE. For instance, a seven-year-old moving from Finland, where formal schooling begins later, may still be eligible to join Grade 1 or Year 2, despite potentially lacking some classroom skills compared to their peers. Schools are expected to provide support, such as beginner-level literacy and numeracy activities, ensuring a smoother transition.

### Transitioning Between Grades

For children who may have been placed in lower grades overseas, the guidelines specify that placement for those from Pre-KG through KG2 will be determined primarily by age. From Grade 1 onwards, the transfer certificate will play a crucial role. If there is a significant discrepancy between the grade shown on the transfer certificate and the UAE's age-grade chart, the school must inform KHDA prior to confirming the child's placement.

Children who began their education later or have been moved down in grades may still qualify for age-appropriate placement, along with the necessary learning support. The guidelines also suggest that children who are ahead of their age group will be placed according to age until KG2 or Year 1. From Grade 1 onward, schools can consider the transfer certificate to support placement above the typical age group, but must seek KHDA approval for such cases.

### Implications for Parents and Students

The introduction of these placement guidelines is significant for parents navigating the admissions process for their children in Dubai. With the focus on ensuring that children are placed in grades appropriate to their age and developmental stage, families can expect a more streamlined and transparent admissions process. However, it is essential for parents to be aware of the revised cut-off dates and how they may affect their child's entry into the school system.

Overall, the KHDA's new guidelines reflect a commitment to providing quality education tailored to the needs of students, ensuring that each child has the opportunity to thrive in a supportive learning environment. As the 2026-27 academic year approaches, families are encouraged to familiarize themselves with these changes to make informed decisions regarding their children's education in Dubai.
