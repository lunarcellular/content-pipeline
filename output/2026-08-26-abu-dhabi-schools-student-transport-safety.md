## Introduction

[Abu Dhabi](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Abu%20Dhabi)'s Department of Education and Knowledge (ADEK) has reaffirmed that [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) in the emirate are fully accountable for the safety of students during transport. This decision comes as part of a comprehensive effort to enhance student safety on school buses, mandating that schools either directly operate transport services or employ approved external operators.

## New Regulations on Transport Safety

Under the new regulations, all schools are required to ensure safe transport for students, particularly those with additional educational needs. The ADEK emphasised that schools must also provide necessary support services to facilitate access to these transport options. In cases where transport cannot be arranged, schools must seek an exemption from the Integrated Transport Centre (ITC).

ADEK's School Transport Policy Guide outlines the responsibilities of schools and transport operators, establishing a framework of accountability. The guide mandates strict adherence to the regulations set forth by the ITC, which governs all school transport services in Abu Dhabi.

## Accountability Measures and Violations

The new policy includes a detailed list of violations that schools and transport operators may incur. ADEK has identified 56 specific violations, which carry penalties ranging from modest fines to significant financial consequences. For instance, failing to provide an adequate number of buses can lead to fines that escalate with repeated infractions. 

The regulations also specify responsibilities for various stakeholders involved in school transport. Schools must appoint a transport officer and ensure that every bus carrying students under the age of 11 has a designated supervisor. Non-compliance can result in fines ranging from Dh5,000 to Dh20,000, depending on the nature of the violation.

## Implications for Parents and Schools

For parents, these regulations underscore the importance of safety in school transport. The accountability placed on schools means that parents can expect a higher standard of care when it comes to transporting their children. This heightened scrutiny not only ensures safer journeys but also encourages schools to invest in better transport solutions and training for staff involved in student transport.

Moreover, the penalties associated with non-compliance may prompt schools to review their transport policies and practices more rigorously. This could lead to improved communications with parents regarding transport arrangements and safety measures, ultimately fostering a more transparent relationship between schools and families.

## Conclusion

The ADEK's initiative to hold schools accountable for student transport safety marks a significant step towards enhancing the overall educational experience in Abu Dhabi. As these regulations take effect, families can look forward to safer transport solutions that prioritise the welfare of students. The emphasis on compliance and accountability is not just a regulatory measure; it reflects a commitment to fostering a secure and supportive educational environment for all students in the emirate.
