In a significant shift in educational policy, the UAE Ministry of Education has announced that pupils from kindergarten to grade two in public [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) will not have any homework during the upcoming academic year. Additionally, students in grade six will rely solely on traditional learning materials like books and notebooks for key subjects, foregoing digital screens entirely.

## New Homework Policy for Early Learners

The decision to eliminate homework for young children aims to foster a better balance between school and family life, allowing for more quality time at home. The Ministry believes that this approach will not only improve learning outcomes but also encourage daily reading habits among young pupils. The rationale behind this policy stems from a study involving 2,500 students across nine public schools, which indicated a positive correlation between reduced screen time and enhanced academic performance. According to the findings, classrooms without digital devices saw significant improvements in students' retention rates and overall behaviour.

## Restrictions on Digital Learning in Grade Six

For pupils in grade six, the new regulations stipulate that digital screens will not be used in subjects such as English, Arabic, science, and mathematics. This initiative is part of a broader strategy to address concerns about excessive screen time and its effects on cognitive development. The Ministry has noted that limiting digital tool usage has yielded improvements not only in academic performance but also in student interactions and classroom discipline.

## Additional Educational Reforms

These changes come alongside other educational reforms for the new academic year. The Ministry plans to introduce diagnostic assessments that will help teachers better understand students' academic levels and identify learning gaps early on. This data-driven approach aims to tailor instructional methodologies to meet the diverse needs of pupils.

Furthermore, the assessment framework for students in grades nine to twelve will also undergo changes, with a balanced distribution between central exams and school-based assessments, each accounting for 50% of the overall evaluation. This marks a shift from the previous year, where school-based assessments constituted 70% of the evaluation.

## Context and Implications for Parents

The decision to revise homework policies and assessment methods reflects a growing trend in the UAE toward prioritising holistic education over traditional rote learning. For parents, these changes may alleviate some of the pressures associated with homework and screen time, particularly for younger children. The focus on enhancing classroom engagement without the distraction of digital devices could lead to a more effective learning environment.

As public schools prepare to welcome over 26,700 new pupils this academic year, the introduction of 11 new schools and educational complexes signifies a commitment to expanding educational opportunities within the UAE. Parents should stay informed about these developments, as they may influence their choices regarding school enrolment and educational support at home.

Overall, the Ministry's initiatives signal a transformative approach to education that values the well-being of students, aiming to create a more balanced and effective learning experience.
