## New Homework Policies for Young Pupils

The UAE education system is undergoing significant changes for the upcoming academic year, particularly regarding homework and the use of digital screens in classrooms. Beginning this September, young pupils from kindergarten to grade two in public [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) will not be assigned any homework, a move aimed at enhancing their learning experience and promoting better family interactions.

The Ministry of Education has stated that this initiative is designed to foster a more balanced approach to learning, allowing children to engage more freely with family life while also encouraging reading habits at home. This approach aligns with recent studies that suggest a reduction in homework can lead to improved academic performance and student well-being.

## Limitations on Screen Time

In a related development, sixth-grade students in public schools will not use digital screens for core subjects such as English, Arabic, science, and maths. Instead, these students will be encouraged to rely on traditional learning materials like books and notebooks. This policy stems from a study conducted on 2,500 pupils across nine public schools, which revealed that classes without digital devices showed notable improvements in both academic performance and classroom behaviour.

The Minister of Education highlighted that reducing screen time has led to better retention rates, improved discipline, and enhanced interpersonal relationships among students. This shift reflects a broader strategy to re-evaluate how technology is integrated into the learning process, especially for younger students.

## Broader Educational Reforms

Beyond these specific changes, the Ministry has introduced additional reforms for the new academic year. These include the implementation of diagnostic tests aimed at identifying students' academic levels and learning gaps early in the year. This proactive approach allows teachers to tailor their lesson plans and instructional methods to better support individual student needs.

Furthermore, the assessment methodology for grades nine to twelve will be revised to balance central exams and school-based assessments, each accounting for 50 per cent of the total evaluation. This change aims to provide schools with greater flexibility in how they assess student performance.

The Ministry of Education also announced the addition of 11 new schools and education complexes, contributing to a total enrolment of over 26,700 new pupils in public schools. The overall number of students enrolled in public schools across the UAE is approximately 1,277,000.

## Implications for Parents

These recent changes in the UAE education system are likely to have a profound impact on parents and their children. The elimination of homework for young pupils may ease the daily pressures families face while encouraging children to cultivate a love for reading and learning outside the classroom. Moreover, the restrictions on screen time should reassure parents concerned about the negative effects of excessive device use on their children's cognitive and social development.

As schools prepare to welcome students back to in-person learning, parents should remain informed about these new policies and their potential effects on their children's educational experience. Schools are expected to implement these changes effectively, all while maintaining the flexibility to adapt to any future shifts in learning formats as necessary.

In summary, the upcoming academic year promises to bring significant changes to the educational landscape in the UAE, with a strong focus on enhancing learning outcomes and well-being for students. Parents should consider how these adjustments align with their educational priorities and support their children's development within this evolving framework.
