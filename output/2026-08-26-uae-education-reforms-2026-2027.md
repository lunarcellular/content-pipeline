## New Education Reforms for UAE Schools

The Ministry of Education has announced a comprehensive set of reforms for UAE [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) that will take effect in the 2026-2027 academic year. These reforms include the elimination of homework for students in kindergarten through grade 2, as well as a new assessment framework designed to better align educational practices with the needs of modern learners.

### Shift to Skill-Based Learning

A central goal of the reforms is to transition from traditional theoretical learning to a more skill-based educational approach. This shift is part of the broader implementation of the National Education Charter, which aims to enhance educational outcomes and ensure that learning is more applicable to real-world situations. The Minister of Education highlighted that a significant component of this new approach will focus on how students can apply what they have learned, rather than simply recalling information.

### New Assessment Policies

One of the most notable changes involves the introduction of a 50-50 assessment system for students. This will see a combination of centralised tests and school-based assessments for students in grades 9 to 12. The aim is to create a more balanced evaluation of student performance. Moreover, the Project-Based Learning and Assessment (PBLA) model will be introduced for students in grade 7, covering key subjects including English, Arabic, Mathematics, and Science.

### Reassessing Digital Tool Use

In line with the new educational strategies, the Ministry of Education is also reassessing the use of digital tools in classrooms. Concerns have been raised regarding the impact of excessive screen time on learning outcomes. Studies have indicated that students who limit their use of digital devices perform better academically and develop stronger interpersonal relationships. Consequently, for certain subjects, such as Arabic and Mathematics, students in grade 6 will solely use textbooks and traditional learning materials this academic year.

### Supporting a Growing Student Population

As the student population continues to grow, with approximately 1.277 million students expected to return to school this year, the Ministry of Education has announced the opening of 11 new schools and educational complexes. Additionally, 8 million textbooks will be distributed to support the educational needs of students. These measures aim to accommodate the increasing number of students and ensure that adequate resources are available.

### Professional Development for Educators

To complement these changes, the Ministry is rolling out 80 training programmes for teaching staff to better equip them with the skills needed to implement the new curriculum effectively. Furthermore, school leadership will also receive targeted training throughout the academic year, ensuring that educational leaders are aligned with the new vision for UAE schools.

### Conclusion: What This Means for Parents

The newly announced reforms signify a substantial shift in the educational landscape of the UAE, particularly regarding assessment methods and the role of homework. Parents can expect a more balanced approach to education that prioritises practical skills and collaborative learning. The emphasis on reducing homework for younger students may alleviate some pressure on families, allowing for more balanced daily routines. 

As these reforms are implemented, parents will need to stay informed about how these changes may affect their children's education and overall academic experience. The Ministry of Education's commitment to enhancing educational outcomes signals a promising direction for the future of schooling in the UAE.
