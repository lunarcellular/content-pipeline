## Introduction of New Device Policy in UAE Schools

Starting in the 2026-2027 academic year, [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) in the UAE will adopt a new policy aimed at limiting the use of electronic devices in core subjects. This initiative will see the reinstatement of traditional learning tools, such as books, notebooks, and pens, for subjects including Arabic, English, mathematics, and science.

## Rationale Behind the Change

This decision follows a comprehensive review of the impact that extensive device usage has had on student learning and classroom experiences. The review highlighted concerns regarding writing, reading, language development, concentration, and the relationship between thinking and writing. The Ministry of Education has indicated that the shift back to paper-based materials is intended to strengthen foundational skills while promoting a more balanced learning environment.

The Minister of Education noted that this approach does not signify a total withdrawal from technology. Instead, it aims to ensure that digital tools are employed judiciously, enhancing educational outcomes rather than dominating the learning experience. Tablets, computers, and other digital resources will still be utilised in contexts where they can genuinely benefit the educational process.

## Insights from the Pilot Programme

An earlier pilot programme conducted during the previous academic year provided valuable insights. A select group of students transitioned from device-dependent learning back to traditional materials. This trial revealed notable improvements in academic performance in various subjects, alongside enhanced student interaction and behaviour within the classroom environment. 

Such findings suggest that a more measured integration of technology into the learning process could yield significant benefits. The Ministry's intention is to reserve electronic devices for specific educational activities that truly necessitate their use, thereby helping to maintain student focus and bolster essential literacy and numeracy skills.

## Implications for Parents and Students

For parents evaluating educational options for their children in the UAE, this new device policy may influence their choices significantly. Schools that adopt this approach may appeal to families who value a traditional educational framework that prioritises foundational skills and personal interaction over excessive reliance on technology.

Additionally, the move towards a more regulated use of classroom technology could reassure parents concerned about the potential distractions and learning barriers posed by constant device availability. This policy not only aims to enhance the quality of education but also reflects a broader recognition of the need for balance in the modern learning environment.

## Conclusion

The forthcoming changes to device usage in UAE schools mark a significant shift in educational policy, prioritising core learning skills and fostering a more interactive classroom atmosphere. As schools prepare for these adjustments, parents can anticipate a renewed focus on traditional educational methods that may better support their children's academic development and well-being. 

This initiative underscores the evolving landscape of education in the UAE, where the integration of technology is being thoughtfully reconsidered to ensure that it serves the best interests of students.
