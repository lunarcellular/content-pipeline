## Significant Changes in UAE Education Policy

The UAE Ministry of Education has announced transformative changes for the upcoming academic year, significantly affecting students in kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2. Starting August 31, these students will no longer have homework assigned, a decision aimed at enhancing their educational experience and fostering a healthier balance between school and home life.

This policy shift is part of a broader initiative to reduce classroom screen time. By the 2026-2027 academic year, the Ministry will expand this reduction to include all grade 6 students in public [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools), reflecting an ongoing commitment to improving learning outcomes and overall student well-being.

## The Rationale Behind the Changes

The Ministry's decision to eliminate homework for younger students is grounded in research indicating that reducing screen time and homework can lead to better learning, behaviour, and social interactions among pupils. Emphasising the importance of early literacy, the new approach encourages daily home reading instead of traditional homework assignments. This shift is designed to cultivate a love for learning rather than subjecting young children to the pressures of nightly worksheets.

In explaining the rationale, the Ministry highlighted that young learners thrive in environments that prioritise play, sleep, and family time. By replacing traditional homework with reading activities, the Ministry aims to foster a lower-pressure routine that still supports crucial skill development.

## Expanding Digital Tools Project

Alongside the homework policy, the Ministry is also expanding its digital tools project, which will apply to grade 6 students as part of the new initiative. This project aims to balance the use of technology in classrooms while reinforcing fundamental academic skills in subjects such as Arabic, English, Mathematics, and Science. 

The Ministry’s approach is informed by a study involving 2,500 students across nine public schools, which revealed that students who engaged in classes without digital devices experienced nearly double the attainment in terms of academic performance compared to those who used screens. This finding underscores the Ministry's commitment to creating calmer and more focused classroom environments.

## Future Education Reforms

The changes come as part of a larger framework of reforms under the National Education Charter, which includes a new assessment model for students in grades 9 to 12 and the introduction of a Project-Based Learning model for grade 7. These reforms signify a shift from traditional assessment methods toward a model that prioritises the application of knowledge over rote memorisation.

Moreover, the Ministry has initiated a second phase of Arabic language skills assessment, targeting approximately 30,000 Grade 1 students across both public and private schools. This step aims to reinforce Arabic language education as a core national priority while supporting early skill development.

## Implications for Parents and Students

For parents, these changes represent a significant shift in how early education is approached in the UAE. The elimination of homework for younger children can alleviate pressures associated with after-school workloads and promote a more balanced family life. Additionally, the focus on reading over worksheets aligns with many parents' desires for a more engaging and less stressful educational environment for their children.

As these policies take effect, parents will need to consider how they can best support their children’s learning at home, particularly through reading initiatives that will now take precedence. 

Overall, the Ministry of Education's reforms signal a progressive move towards enhancing the educational landscape in the UAE, aiming to cultivate a generation of learners who are not only academically proficient but also well-rounded and engaged in their communities.
