## Overview of Changes in UAE Schools

As the new academic year 2026-27 approaches, over 1.277 million students are set to return to more than 1,180 public and private [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) across the UAE. This year marks a pivotal moment in the educational landscape, with the Ministry of Education implementing a series of transformative changes aimed at enhancing the learning experience for students, teachers, and parents alike.

## Key Initiatives and Policies

The Ministry is activating the National Education Charter, a unified vision for education that aims to cultivate essential competencies and values within the student population. This initiative, launched by national leaders, seeks to integrate the Charter’s principles into daily school activities, emphasizing collaboration among schools, families, and the community.

To support these objectives, the Ministry will launch a back-to-school campaign themed “Guided by the Charter, Building Tomorrow.” This campaign will focus on embedding the Charter’s values into school practices and enhancing family engagement in the educational process.

### Changes to Homework and Assessment

One of the most significant changes involves the removal of traditional homework for students from Kindergarten through Grade 2. This decision reflects a growing understanding of young children's developmental needs, aiming to foster a healthier balance between school responsibilities and family life. Instead of homework, families will be encouraged to promote daily reading at home, which is expected to enhance early literacy and cognitive skills.

In terms of assessment, the Ministry has announced a shift in the weighting for Group A subjects in Cycle 3 to a 50:50 split between school-based assessments and central assessments. This new balance is designed to provide a more accurate reflection of a student's performance throughout the year while maintaining a consistent national framework.

### Advancements in Arabic Language Skills

The Ministry will also introduce the second phase of its Arabic language skills assessment, which will encompass all Grade 1 students in both public and private schools. This initiative aims to establish a clear baseline of students' Arabic language proficiency, enabling schools to tailor support from the earliest stages of education.

### Digital Tool Regulation

Another noteworthy development is the expansion of the 'Regulating the Use of Digital Tools in Education' project. Following a study that highlighted improved academic performance and student behaviour in non-digital classrooms, the Ministry will now implement this policy for all Grade 6 students in public schools. The intention is to strike a balance between the use of technology and the reinforcement of fundamental learning skills, ensuring that digital tools serve as a support mechanism rather than a distraction.

## Project-Based Learning Initiatives

The Ministry is also expanding its Project-Based Learning and Assessment initiative, which will now involve over 129,000 students across 366 schools nationwide. A new integrated project for Grade 7 students will combine subjects such as Arabic, English, mathematics, and science into a single task, promoting interdisciplinary learning and practical application of knowledge.

## Implications for Parents

These substantial changes in the UAE education system signal a shift towards a more holistic and integrated approach to learning. For parents, this means a re-evaluation of the traditional concepts of homework and assessment, alongside new opportunities to engage more actively in their children’s educational journey. The focus on early literacy, the regulation of digital tools, and project-based learning signifies a move towards fostering critical thinking and practical skills that are essential for future success.

As parents explore educational options for their children in light of these changes, understanding the implications of these new policies will be crucial. The emphasis on national identity and competencies, coupled with a commitment to improving educational outcomes, reflects the UAE's ongoing efforts to enhance its education system in line with global standards.

In summary, the 2026-27 academic year promises to bring a fresh perspective to education in the UAE, with initiatives designed to empower students and foster a collaborative learning environment.
