## Significant Policy Changes in UAE Schools

The Ministry of Education (MoE) has announced pivotal changes to school policies that will take effect in the 2026-2027 academic year, primarily aimed at enhancing the learning environment for young students. The new regulations will impact children in kindergarten, grade 1, and grade 2, as well as all grade 6 students in public [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) and private institutions managed by the ministry.

## No Homework for Early Learners

Starting from August 31, 2026, students in the specified age groups will no longer have homework assigned. The Ministry of Education has made this decision based on research indicating that early learners benefit from reduced stress and increased family engagement, which are critical for their development. Instead of traditional homework, the ministry is promoting daily home reading, which is designed to foster early literacy and essential language skills without the pressures associated with standard homework.

The rationale behind this change is to create a more balanced approach between school and home life, allowing families to spend quality time together while still encouraging academic growth. The ministry believes that this shift aligns with early childhood developmental needs, ultimately cultivating a lifelong love for learning rather than inducing burnout from excessive worksheets.

## Reduction of Classroom Screen Time

In conjunction with the no-homework policy, the MoE will also implement measures to reduce screen time in classrooms. This initiative will extend to all grade 6 students, focusing on subjects such as Arabic, English, Mathematics, and Science. Current research has demonstrated that excessive screen use can hinder student learning and social interactions, leading to a push for a more traditional learning approach, which has shown to yield better academic results.

Previous studies conducted by the ministry across multiple public schools revealed that students who engaged less with digital tools performed significantly better. The findings indicated a marked improvement in the academic achievements of students identified as needing additional support, as well as overall classroom dynamics, which were described as calmer and more focused.

## Broader Educational Reforms

These changes are part of a more extensive educational reform effort under the new National Education Charter. This charter includes a comprehensive assessment model that combines centralised and school-based testing for students in grades 9 to 12. Additionally, a Project-Based Learning model will be introduced for students in grade 7, emphasising practical applications of knowledge rather than rote memorisation.

Furthermore, the ministry has initiated a second phase of an Arabic language skills assessment programme, targeting approximately 30,000 students in various public and private schools. This initiative aims to reinforce Arabic as a core subject and support early skill development, reflecting the nation's commitment to preserving its linguistic heritage.

## Implications for Parents

For parents navigating the educational landscape in the UAE, these policy changes present both challenges and opportunities. The elimination of homework for younger students may alleviate some stress and allow for more flexible family routines, but it also raises questions about how academic progress will be monitored in the absence of traditional assignments. 

Moreover, the reduced screen time policy suggests a shift back to conventional teaching methods, which might resonate positively with parents concerned about the over-reliance on technology in their children's education. However, parents will need to remain engaged in their children's reading habits at home to complement the new policies effectively.

As these reforms roll out, parents should stay informed and consider how these changes align with their educational expectations and the learning styles of their children. The focus on foundational skills and less pressure on young learners indicates a significant shift in the educational approach within the UAE, aiming for a more holistic development of students.
