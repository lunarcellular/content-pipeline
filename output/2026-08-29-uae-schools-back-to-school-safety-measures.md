## UAE Schools Set for New Academic Year

As the new academic year approaches, UAE [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) are implementing essential safety measures and training to ensure a smooth transition for students and parents. More than 1,180 public and private schools across the UAE are set to reopen their doors on August 31, welcoming over 1.277 million students. This year's return includes a comprehensive focus on traffic safety and security around school zones, which is particularly important as parents and students prepare for the increased activity typical of the back-to-school period.

## Traffic Safety Initiatives

In anticipation of the school rush, the [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai) Police have organised a series of training and awareness courses specifically for security personnel in 35 schools. This initiative aims to enhance understanding of traffic safety rules and guidelines, which is crucial during peak drop-off and pick-up hours. Security guards are being familiarised with traffic signs and regulations, as well as undergoing hands-on training that simulates real-life traffic accidents. This training ensures that they are well-prepared to manage traffic flow and respond to emergencies effectively.

Moreover, the UAE Ministry of Interior has announced a one-day traffic safety campaign coinciding with the start of the school year, which will allow motorists to have four black points removed from their driving licences if they maintain safe driving practices. To bolster safety measures, Dubai Police will deploy 253 patrols and 28 drones around schools, while the Roads and Transport Authority will increase bus frequencies on over 30 routes and add taxis in school zones. Similar safety boosts will occur in [Abu Dhabi](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Abu%20Dhabi), [Sharjah](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Sharjah), Ajman, and Ras Al Khaimah, with increased patrols and management of traffic flow around schools.

## Phased Return to School

This year, schools in the UAE are adopting a phased return-to-school plan that includes shorter hours for younger students during the first week. To facilitate a smoother transition, staggered drop-off and pick-up times will be implemented, allowing for better management of student arrivals and departures. Parents have begun receiving communications from schools detailing these arrangements, highlighting the importance of planning for the new academic year.

For parents working in the government sector, there is good news: flexible working hours will be permitted on the first day of school. This provision will allow parents to adjust their schedules to accommodate their children's school timings, ensuring that they can safely take their children to and from school without added stress.

## Changes in Homework Policy

In a significant change to the education system, the UAE's Minister of Education has announced that students in kindergarten through Year 2 at public schools will no longer be assigned homework. This alteration aims to reduce academic pressure on young learners and is part of a broader initiative to enhance educational practices across the UAE. Additionally, fresh evaluations will be implemented for all public and private schools licensed by the Ministry of Education.

## Focus on Professional Development

To support these changes, a specialised training week was conducted for school leaders, teachers, and support staff. This initiative included around 80 targeted programmes and hybrid workshops aimed at enhancing the skills of over 24,000 educational professionals. The training encompassed a dedicated development programme for 1,700 school leaders, including 500 principals and 1,200 vice principals, ensuring that they are well-equipped to implement the new guidelines effectively.

## Conclusion: What This Means for Parents

The preparations for the new academic year signify a strong commitment from educational authorities in the UAE to prioritise student safety and wellbeing. For parents, this means a well-organised return to school, with enhanced safety measures and flexible working arrangements that can alleviate some of the stress typically associated with the start of the school year. As schools implement these changes, parents can expect a more structured and supportive environment for their children, fostering a positive educational experience from the outset.
