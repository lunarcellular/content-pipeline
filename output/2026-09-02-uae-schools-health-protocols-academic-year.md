## UAE Schools Reinforce Health Protocols for New Academic Year

As the new academic year commences, [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) across the UAE are emphasising the importance of health and safety protocols. With the return to classrooms on August 31, parents are reminded to keep unwell children at home to prevent the spread of illness and ensure a conducive learning environment.

Schools are advocating for parents to monitor their children's health closely, particularly during this transitional period. Symptoms that warrant a child staying home include fever, cough, vomiting, and diarrhoea. The goal is to promote recovery while minimising disruption in classrooms, with a focus on cooperation between families and educational institutions.

## Health Guidance from Authorities

The health and safety practices implemented in schools are in line with recommendations from the [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai) Health Authority and the Knowledge and Human Development Authority. Schools are reinforcing the message that children learn best when they are healthy, and clear guidelines have been established regarding when students should remain at home.

In particular, schools in Dubai have specified that children should not return until they are fever-free for at least 24 hours without the use of fever-reducing medications. Additionally, those who have experienced vomiting or diarrhoea are advised to wait at least 48 hours after symptoms subside before returning to school. 

This proactive approach is designed not only to protect the individual child but also to safeguard the wider school community.

## Vaccination Initiatives and Health Measures

In conjunction with illness prevention strategies, schools are continuing their vaccination initiatives. Many institutions have begun vaccination drives ahead of the school year, enabling parents to provide vaccination records for their children. This allows schools to identify any gaps in immunisation and facilitate necessary vaccinations with parental consent.

Vaccination programmes are tailored to various age groups and health requirements, ensuring that students are protected against prevalent diseases. For example, a vaccination for influenza is typically scheduled for September and October, emphasising the importance of staying up to date with immunisations.

Schools are also enhancing their on-campus health measures, including improved cleanliness, ventilation, and hygiene routines. The focus on maintaining a clean environment is critical, as it plays a significant role in reducing the likelihood of illness transmission among students.

## Collaboration Between Schools and Families

Effective communication between parents and schools is deemed essential in managing student health. Schools are encouraging families to engage proactively, particularly when children exhibit symptoms. This collaboration is vital in fostering a culture of health awareness and responsibility within the school community.

Parents are advised to consult with healthcare professionals when necessary, especially if they are uncertain about their child's symptoms. By working together, schools and families can ensure that children receive the care they need while maintaining a healthy educational environment.

As the academic year progresses, the emphasis on health protocols will remain a priority for schools across the UAE. The commitment to protecting student health not only supports individual well-being but also enhances the overall educational experience for all students.

In summary, as the new school year unfolds, the collective effort of parents and schools to uphold health protocols will be crucial in nurturing a safe and productive learning atmosphere. This shared responsibility will ultimately contribute to the success of students and the broader school community.
