## New Measures in UAE Schools

Starting from tomorrow, [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) across the UAE will implement new regulations aimed at reducing the reliance on digital tools for Grade 6 students. This initiative, outlined in the “Guide to Reducing the Use of Digital Tools in Learning,” marks a significant shift back to traditional educational methods, focusing on textbook and notebook usage.

## Restrictions on Digital Devices

Under the new guidelines, Grade 6 students will no longer be allowed to bring laptops or other digital devices to school. This decision applies specifically to both general and advanced tracks within the education system. It is part of a broader effort to refocus classroom learning on direct interaction, handwriting practices, and the use of physical learning materials. Schools have communicated these changes to parents, urging them not to send laptops with their children for the remainder of the academic year.

While the use of laptops in classrooms will be curtailed, it is important to note that technology is not being completely banned. Instead, digital devices will be permitted for targeted use at home, specifically when required for educational assignments or accessing specific digital resources. This move is intended to encourage more purposeful engagement with technology rather than allowing unrestricted screen time during school hours.

## Emphasis on Traditional Learning Methods

The reinstatement of textbooks and notebooks in the classroom is designed to foster a learning environment centred around active participation. Students will be encouraged to read from physical books, take notes by hand, and engage in collaborative activities with peers. This approach aims to enhance critical thinking and interpersonal skills, which can sometimes be diminished in a heavily digital learning environment.

The initiative also seeks to involve families more directly in managing their children's technology use at home. Parents are expected to support their children by setting clear guidelines for when and how digital devices can be utilised for educational purposes. This collaboration is seen as essential for the successful implementation of the new measures.

## Preparing for Digital Access

In tandem with these changes, government schools have begun identifying students who lack ministry-issued laptops. This proactive approach is part of a broader strategy to ensure that students in higher grades, where technology is integrated into learning, are adequately equipped. Schools are gathering information from families to facilitate this process, with a focus on ensuring that all students have access to necessary digital resources when applicable.

This dual approach—restricting digital device use in classrooms while ensuring access to technology at home—aims to create a balanced educational experience. It reflects a growing recognition of the importance of moderating screen time among students, particularly at a critical learning stage like Grade 6.

## Implications for Parents

For parents navigating the education landscape in the UAE, these changes signal a shift towards a more traditional and perhaps more controlled classroom environment. The emphasis on textbooks may appeal to those concerned about the impact of excessive screen time on their children’s learning and development. However, it also places a greater responsibility on families to monitor and regulate technology use at home.

As schools adapt to these new guidelines, parents may want to engage with educators to understand how these measures will affect their children’s learning experiences. This could include discussions about the types of assignments that may require digital access and how parents can best support their children in achieving their educational goals.

In conclusion, the new regulations being introduced in UAE schools represent a significant shift in educational practices, aiming to balance traditional learning with the responsible use of technology. This approach not only prioritises the immediate educational needs of students but also prepares them for a future in which digital literacy remains vital.
