## Overview of the New Admission Policy

The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) has introduced a new age cut-off policy for school admissions in [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai), effective from the 2026-27 academic year. This significant change will impact children entering Kindergarten and Grade 1, making it essential for parents to be aware of the new guidelines as they prepare for school registration.

## Key Details of the Age Cut-Off Policy

The official cut-off date is now December 31 of the admission year, replacing the previous date of August 31. This adjustment applies to new admissions at [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) and kindergartens commencing their academic year in August or September. Existing students will not be affected by this change. Schools starting their academic year in April will continue to use March 31 as their cut-off date.

This policy was introduced following a recommendation from the Ministry of Education, which aimed to ensure fairness, support educational readiness, and maintain stability in academic progression for students. The decision is grounded in a comprehensive technical review that analysed data from over 39,000 students, alongside international benchmarks and best practices in early education.

## Eligibility Criteria Based on Birth Dates

Under the new policy, eligibility for different educational stages is determined by the child's birth date:

- **Children born between January 1 and December 31, 2023** are eligible for Pre-Kindergarten in various foreign curricula, FS1 in British schools, and Petite Section (PS) in French schools.
- **Children born between January 1 and December 31, 2022** can enter KG1 under the Ministry of Education and American/foreign curricula, FS2 under the British curriculum, and Moyenne Section (MS) in French schools.
- **Children born between January 1 and December 31, 2021** are eligible for KG2 under the Ministry of Education and American/foreign curricula, Year 1 in British schools, and Grande Section (GS) in French schools.
- Additionally, a special provision allows children born between September 1 and December 31, 2021, who were previously ineligible, to register for either KG1 or KG2 in the 2026-27 academic year.
- For children born between January 1 and December 31, 2020, eligibility extends to Grade 1 under the Ministry of Education and American/foreign curricula, Year 2 under the British curriculum, and Cours Préparatoire (CP) in French schools.

## Transitional Measures and Assessments

The policy includes transitional measures for certain birth dates. Specifically, children born between September 1 and December 31 who did not turn three by the start of the school year may be assessed for readiness to join FS1, allowing schools and parents to decide on the most suitable placement. This flexibility aims to accommodate the diverse developmental needs of young learners.

For April-start schools, the previous cut-off date of March 31 remains applicable. The new age cut-off does not automatically guarantee a child’s admission into a specific grade when transferring between schools or curricula. Placement decisions will be based on the last successfully completed grade, ensuring educational continuity and minimal disruption to the student’s learning path.

## Implications for Parents

This updated age cut-off policy is crucial for parents navigating the school admissions process in Dubai. Families must be aware of the eligibility criteria based on their child's birth date to ensure proper registration in the appropriate grade. The emphasis on educational readiness and the flexibility offered by the new policy reflect an understanding of the varied developmental timelines of children, which can ultimately enhance their learning experience.

As the education landscape in Dubai continues to evolve, it is essential for parents to stay informed about changes that can affect their children's educational journey. Understanding these policies will not only aid in the smooth transition into school but also foster a supportive environment for young learners as they embark on their academic journeys.
