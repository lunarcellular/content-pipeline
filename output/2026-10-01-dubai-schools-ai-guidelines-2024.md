## New AI Guidelines for Dubai Schools

In a move aimed at balancing educational innovation with responsible technology use, [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) in [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai) will implement specific guidelines concerning the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in classrooms starting next year. The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) is leading this initiative, emphasising the importance of preparing students for a digital future while ensuring they are not overwhelmed by excessive screen time.

### Balancing Innovation and Safeguards

The KHDA is committed to establishing clear standards that will govern how AI can be integrated into the educational framework. While specific details of the guidelines remain under wraps, key elements are expected to include recommendations for age-appropriate use of technology and safeguards that schools should implement. The aim is to encourage the beneficial use of AI in enhancing learning outcomes while also mitigating the risks associated with prolonged screen exposure.

The KHDA's director general highlighted the necessity of finding a middle ground in technology use, stating that the goal is to foster responsible usage rather than simply limiting access. This approach reflects the increasing recognition of AI's potential to transform educational practices when applied judiciously.

### Addressing Screen Time Concerns

One of the critical aspects of the new guidelines will be the consideration of students' age and developmental stages in determining the appropriateness of AI and technology use in classrooms. The KHDA is also collaborating with other authorities to educate families about screen time, as it is acknowledged that children's technology use extends beyond school hours. This holistic approach aims to address the challenges posed by excessive screen time and its impact on students' overall well-being.

### Enhancing the Educational Landscape

According to recent reports, the number of students enrolled in private schools in Dubai has grown to approximately 406,500, reflecting a 4.6 per cent increase since June. In light of this rising demand for school places, the KHDA is developing a comprehensive education master plan. This plan will ensure that the supply of schools aligns with the population's needs, taking into account curriculum preferences and affordable fee structures.

The plan is also part of Dubai's broader ambition to create a city where most daily needs, including schooling, can be accessed within a 20-minute walk or cycle. This vision aligns with the Dubai 2040 Urban Master Plan, which seeks to improve the quality of life for residents by minimising travel times.

### Preparing for the Future

As part of its long-term strategy, the KHDA is also considering the broader implications of AI in education. In September, measures were approved by the UAE Cabinet to enhance AI's role across various sectors, including education. The government aims to train a significant number of teachers and educators in AI applications for teaching, assessment, and lesson planning.

While the KHDA is likely to provide a framework for AI implementation, it will not dictate the specific AI platforms that schools must adopt. Instead, the focus will be on establishing standards that encourage innovation while ensuring the safety and well-being of students.

### Conclusion

For parents navigating the educational landscape in Dubai, these developments signal a significant shift towards integrating technology in a responsible manner. As schools prepare to adopt new AI guidelines, families can expect a more balanced approach to technology use that prioritises both educational enrichment and the mental health of students. This evolution in educational policy reflects a commitment to preparing children for a future where digital literacy will be paramount, all while fostering a supportive and safe learning environment.
