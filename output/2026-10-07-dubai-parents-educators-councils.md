## Dubai Forms New Councils to Strengthen Educational Collaboration

[Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai)'s Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) has announced the establishment of the city's first Parents Council and Educators Council, alongside the renewal of the Dubai Students Council for the 2026-27 academic year. This initiative is part of Dubai's broader Education 33 (E33) Strategy, which aims to enhance the roles of students, parents, and educators in shaping the educational landscape of the emirate.

The KHDA confirmed that each of the three councils will include 15 parents, 15 teachers and education professionals, and 16 students from Grades 9 to 12 across private [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) in Dubai. This formation marks a significant step towards fostering a collaborative environment in the education sector, encouraging diverse perspectives from all stakeholders involved.

## Councils' Composition and Selection Process

The selection process for these councils was highly competitive, with KHDA receiving 131 nominations for the Students Council, 152 for the Parents Council, and 160 for the Educators Council. The methodology employed ensured fairness and transparency, prioritising representation from Emirati educators and reflecting Dubai's diverse community. 

The establishment of these councils is poised to create formal pathways for communication between KHDA and the educational community, allowing for direct feedback and engagement on various educational policies and initiatives.

## Objectives of the Councils

The renewed Students Council is designed to serve as a direct channel to KHDA. Members will provide insights on how to enhance learning environments, ensuring that students' voices are heard in discussions about educational policy and their overall experiences.

The Dubai Parents Council aims to unify the voices of Emirati and expatriate families, focusing on shared priorities that impact family and student welfare. Its creation provides a structured platform for parents to engage proactively with KHDA, rather than relying solely on feedback through individual schools.

Meanwhile, the Dubai Educators Council will include teachers and educational professionals who will contribute their expertise to discussions on policy development. This council is expected to deliver evidence-based input for programmes that support inclusion and enhance teacher wellbeing across Dubai's private education sector.

## Implications for Parents and Students

The formation of these councils signals a significant shift towards a more inclusive and responsive education system in Dubai. By actively involving parents, educators, and students in meaningful dialogue, KHDA aims to create an educational environment that better meets the needs and aspirations of the community.

This initiative also highlights the importance of collaboration among various stakeholders in the education sector. Parents now have a formal mechanism to express their concerns and suggestions, which could lead to improved educational practices and policies that benefit all students.

The councils are set to operate for one year but members may apply for a second term based on their performance and continuing eligibility. This structure not only encourages commitment but also allows for the evolution of ideas and initiatives over time.

## Conclusion

As Dubai continues to innovate within its educational framework, the establishment of the Parents and Educators Councils represents a forward-thinking approach to governance in education. By prioritising the voices of those directly involved in the learning process, Dubai's educational authorities are setting a precedent for future initiatives aimed at fostering a collaborative and inclusive education system.

Parents and students should view this development as an opportunity to actively engage with the educational landscape, ensuring their perspectives contribute to shaping a better future for education in Dubai.
