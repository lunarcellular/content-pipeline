## New Approval Requirements for Islamic Education Teachers

The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) in [Dubai](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools?location=Dubai) has instituted new regulations requiring Arabic language teachers to obtain separate approval before they can teach Islamic Education to young Muslim children aged four to six. This measure aims to enhance the quality of Islamic Education in early childhood settings, ensuring that educators meet specific qualifications and standards.

These new requirements are outlined in the KHDA's recently released "Guide to Implementing Islamic Education in Early Childhood." The guide specifies that teachers assigned to this subject must possess a KHDA-issued appointment notification that explicitly authorises them to teach Islamic Education, irrespective of their previous teaching credentials or specialisations. This is a significant shift in policy, reflecting the authority's commitment to maintaining high educational standards.

## Teacher Qualifications and Approval Process

Under the new guidelines, the minimum qualification for teachers responsible for Islamic Education in early childhood education is set at Level 3. For those working in [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) with the same age group, the requirement is Level 6. Acceptable qualifications include Islamic Education, Early Childhood Education, Arabic, and related disciplines. Additionally, all teachers assigned to Islamic Education must be Muslim and demonstrate adequate subject knowledge, as specified by the Ministry of Education curriculum.

Educational institutions must verify the qualifications and competence of their teachers through approved recruitment processes. Furthermore, if schools are unable to find a qualified teacher, they must seek KHDA approval for any alternative arrangements they propose.

## Monitoring and Compliance

To ensure compliance with the new educational standards, institutions must implement the prescribed learning framework and outcomes approved by the Ministry of Education. This includes documenting lesson plans, learning resources, and assessment records to track children's progress towards achieving the designated learning outcomes. The guidelines also emphasise the importance of appropriate teaching and assessment methods, tailored to the developmental stages of young children.

Educational institutions are required to maintain comprehensive records that demonstrate how they monitor progress, including any necessary accommodations for children of determination. This reflects a broader commitment to inclusivity within the educational framework.

## Language Instruction Requirements

The guide delineates specific language instruction requirements for Islamic Education based on the children's backgrounds. Arab Muslim children must receive instruction in Arabic, while non-Arab Muslim children may be taught in the language of instruction approved by their educational institution. For parents of non-Arab passport holders, there is an option to request placement in an Arabic-language teaching group, depending on the institution's discretion.

This flexibility allows educational institutions to organise students into groups that best meet their language learning needs, either in the same classroom or separate classrooms. However, if one teacher instructs both groups simultaneously, they must possess strong proficiency in both Arabic and the other language of instruction.

## Implications for Parents and Educational Institutions

These new KHDA regulations underscore a significant shift in the educational landscape for early childhood Islamic Education in Dubai. For parents, this means a heightened assurance that their children will learn from qualified educators who meet stringent standards. The requirement for separate approvals also aims to foster a more structured and effective educational environment for young learners.

As schools adapt to these changes, parents may want to inquire about their chosen institutions' compliance with these new standards, particularly regarding the qualifications of teachers assigned to Islamic Education. This proactive engagement can help parents make informed decisions about their children's education and ensure they receive a suitable and high-quality learning experience. 

Overall, these developments signal Dubai's ongoing commitment to enhancing educational quality and ensuring that the foundations of learning for young children are robust and impactful.
