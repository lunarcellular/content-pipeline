## New Guidelines for Students with Diabetes in UAE Schools

In a significant move to support students with diabetes, the UAE Ministry of Education has introduced new guidelines that offer essential accommodations during examinations and daily school activities. These measures are part of a broader initiative aimed at ensuring that students with chronic health conditions can fully participate in school life alongside their peers.

According to the guidelines, students diagnosed with diabetes are allowed to use blood glucose monitors and mobile phones equipped with glucose sensors during exams. They can also carry snacks and water into exam halls, providing the necessary support for managing their condition effectively. If a student experiences fluctuations in blood sugar levels during an examination, the exam timer must be paused to accommodate the time lost.

## Individual Healthcare Plans Required

Every student with diabetes is required to have an individual healthcare plan, which must be approved by their treating physician. This plan should address key areas including glucose monitoring schedules and insulin dosages. Parents are responsible for submitting this healthcare plan and any relevant medical reports at the beginning of the academic year. [Schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) are expected to maintain accurate records of students with chronic health conditions and ensure that all teachers are informed about these students' needs.

The guidelines detail eight specific requirements that must be included in each healthcare plan. These include provisions for students to check their blood sugar levels at any time, eat food and drink water as needed, and use toilet facilities without having to seek permission repeatedly. Furthermore, schools must ensure that students are able to sit in locations where their wellbeing can be easily monitored by teachers.

## Emphasis on Privacy and Support

The Ministry of Education has underscored the importance of privacy for students with diabetes. Teachers are advised to avoid discussing students' health conditions in front of their classmates, thereby protecting their dignity and ensuring a more supportive environment. Additionally, students should not be penalised for absences related to medical treatment, fostering a culture of understanding and care within the school community.

The guidelines also highlight the necessity for schools to prepare for emergencies by keeping fast-acting sources of sugar readily available in classrooms. Teachers are instructed to document any medical incidents involving students with diabetes and communicate these occurrences with nursing staff and parents.

## Participation in Physical Activities

To promote inclusivity, the Ministry encourages schools to support students with diabetes in participating in physical activities rather than excluding them. Prior to engaging in exercise, blood glucose levels should be checked, and students should have access to snacks and hydration. Moreover, teachers need to monitor students during and after physical activities, as there can be delayed reactions in blood sugar levels.

For school trips and outdoor activities, specific protocols must be followed to ensure the safety and wellbeing of students with diabetes. These include notifying parents and school nursing staff in advance, ensuring a trained individual accompanies the student, and safeguarding medical equipment from extreme temperatures.

## Conclusion

The introduction of these guidelines marks a pivotal step in accommodating the needs of students with diabetes in UAE schools. By fostering a supportive environment, the Ministry of Education aims to ensure that all students can thrive academically and socially, regardless of their health conditions. This initiative not only benefits students but also provides peace of mind for parents, knowing that their children’s health needs will be respected and accommodated in the educational setting. As these policies are implemented, it will be crucial for schools to adapt and maintain open lines of communication with families to ensure the ongoing success of these measures.
