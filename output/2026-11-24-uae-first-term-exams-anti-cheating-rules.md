## Overview of First-Term Exams in the UAE

Pupils in Grades 3 to 12 across the UAE commence their first-term examinations on Wednesday, 25 November, 2026. The exam period, which runs until 11 December, is set against a backdrop of stringent regulations aimed at curbing cheating and ensuring a fair examination environment. These measures reflect a commitment to academic integrity within the educational framework of the UAE.

## Examination Details

The first-term examinations will cater to students from both government and private [schools](https://www.schoolsery.com/schools) following the Ministry of Education's curriculum. For Grades 3 and 4, assessments will be conducted through paper-based tests, while students in Grades 5 to 12 will have the option of paper-based or electronic assessments, depending on their specific subjects. Notably, all pupils are required to attend school in person during this examination period.

Grade 12 students are particularly affected by the new regulations. They are instructed to bring their laptops for electronic assessments, with the examination schedule for this grade starting with mathematics on the first day, followed by English and chemistry. Subsequent subjects include physics, Arabic, Islamic education, social studies, and biology, culminating on 11 December.

## Strict Anti-Cheating Measures

The newly implemented anti-cheating policies impose several strict rules on student conduct during examinations. Students are prohibited from bringing unauthorised electronic devices, such as mobile phones and smartwatches, into examination rooms. Other banned practices include exchanging answers, leaking examination questions, and any form of impersonation.

For students caught cheating for the first time, the repercussions are significant. They will receive a zero in the subject concerned, lose 12 conduct marks, and will not be allowed to take make-up examinations. In addition, parents or guardians will be summoned to discuss the matter with school officials. Repeat offenders face even harsher penalties, including a zero across all subjects and mandatory referral to behavioural intervention programmes.

## Implications for Parents and Students

These developments in the UAE's examination policies underscore a growing emphasis on academic integrity in schools. For parents, this might raise concerns about how such regulations could affect their children's learning environment and exam preparedness.

The implementation of stringent anti-cheating measures may lead to increased anxiety among students, particularly for those who struggle with exam pressure. Parents should be aware of these regulations and encourage their children to approach their studies with integrity and diligence, while also ensuring they are adequately prepared for the examinations.

With the introduction of electronic assessments for certain grades, it is also essential for parents to support their children in understanding the technical requirements and expectations surrounding these formats. This understanding will be crucial for students, especially as many will be required to manage their devices independently during the examination period.

In summary, as the first-term examinations approach, the UAE's educational authorities are taking decisive steps to foster a culture of honesty and accountability. The implications of these regulations will resonate throughout the academic community, shaping the experiences of students and families alike during this critical assessment period.
